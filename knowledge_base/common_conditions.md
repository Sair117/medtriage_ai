# Common Conditions Quick Reference

## Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (Common Cold / Flu)
- Symptoms: runny nose, sore throat, cough, mild fever, body aches, fatigue
- Duration: typically 7-10 days
- Triage: Routine → General Practice
- Escalate if: high fever >39.5°C, difficulty breathing, symptoms >2 weeks, immunocompromised patient

## Migraine
- Symptoms: severe headache (often one-sided), nausea, light/sound sensitivity, visual aura
- Duration: 4-72 hours per episode
- Triage: Routine → Neurology (or GP for first assessment)
- Escalate if: worst headache ever, sudden onset, fever with stiff neck, neurological deficits

## Gastroenteritis
- Symptoms: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps, mild fever
- Duration: 1-3 days typically
- Triage: Routine → General Practice or Gastroenterology
- Escalate if: blood in stool/vomit, severe dehydration, high fever, symptoms >5 days

## Urinary Tract Infection
- Symptoms: burning during urination, frequent urination, lower abdominal pain, cloudy urine
- Triage: Routine → General Practice
- Escalate if: fever with flank pain (possible kidney infection), blood in urine, pregnancy

## Musculoskeletal Strain/Sprain
- Symptoms: pain, swelling, limited range of motion, bruising
- Triage: Routine → Orthopedics or General Practice
- Escalate if: inability to bear weight, visible deformity, numbness/tingling below injury

## Anxiety / Panic Attack
- Symptoms: racing heart, shortness of breath, chest tightness, trembling, sense of dread
- Triage: Routine → Mental Health
- Escalate if: suicidal thoughts, chest pain that could be cardiac, first-ever episode (rule out cardiac cause)

## Allergic Reaction (Mild)
- Symptoms: localized hives, itching, mild swelling, runny nose
- Triage: Routine → General Practice or Dermatology
- Escalate if: throat tightness, difficulty breathing, tongue/lip swelling, widespread reaction

## Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
- Symptoms: often asymptomatic, may have headache, dizziness, nosebleeds
- Triage: Routine → Cardiology or General Practice
- Escalate if: severe headache with confusion, chest pain, vision changes, BP >180/120

## Eczema / Dermatitis
- Symptoms: itchy, red, dry, cracked skin, sometimes with blisters
- Triage: Routine → Dermatology
- Escalate if: signs of skin infection (spreading redness, warmth, pus, fever)

## Asthma Exacerbation
- Symptoms: wheezing, cough, chest tightness, shortness of breath
- Triage: Urgent → Respiratory if not responding to rescue inhaler
- Escalate if: cannot speak in full sentences, blue lips, peak flow <50% of personal best
