# Medical Triage Guidelines

## Triage Levels

### Level 1 — Emergency (Immediate) — ~5-10% of clinic visits
Life-threatening conditions requiring immediate intervention. These patients should call emergency services or go to the ER.

Key criteria (ALL must involve imminent danger to life):
- Cardiac arrest or respiratory arrest
- Severe trauma with uncontrolled bleeding
- Active seizure lasting >5 minutes
- Anaphylaxis with airway compromise (tongue/throat swelling, cannot breathe)
- Clear stroke signs: sudden facial droop + arm weakness + speech difficulty (FAST criteria)
- Crushing chest pain with sweating, radiation to arm/jaw, and shortness of breath
- Complete inability to breathe
- Loss of consciousness
- Suicidal ideation with active plan and means

Important: Individual symptoms like headache, dizziness, or moderate pain are NOT emergencies on their own. Multiple combined red flags with acute onset are what constitute a true emergency.

### Level 2 — Urgent (Within hours, same-day) — ~15-20% of clinic visits
Serious conditions that need prompt attention but are not immediately life-threatening.

Key criteria:
- High fever (>39.5°C / 103°F) especially with confusion or rigors
- Severe pain (8+/10) not controlled by over-the-counter medication
- Head injury with vomiting or drowsiness (but still conscious and alert)
- Moderate allergic reaction (hives spreading but no airway issues)
- Chest pain without classic cardiac features (no sweating, no arm radiation)
- Acute onset severe headache ("worst headache of my life") — but patient is fully alert and oriented
- Fracture with deformity but intact circulation
- Significant wound requiring stitches
- Diabetic emergency with symptoms
- Persistent vomiting >24hrs with signs of dehydration
- Worsening symptoms over several days despite home treatment

### Level 3 — Routine (Scheduled, within days) — ~70-80% of clinic visits
Stable, non-urgent conditions. This is the MOST COMMON category.

Key criteria:
- Headaches (including migraines) without neurological deficits
- Mild to moderate pain (1-7/10)
- Rash or skin irritation lasting days to weeks
- Common cold, mild flu, sore throat, cough
- Chronic joint or back pain
- Minor cuts, bruises, sprains
- Digestive issues (mild nausea, heartburn, constipation)
- Ear pain, mild hearing changes
- Anxiety, stress, insomnia (without crisis)
- General fatigue or malaise
- Routine check-ups and follow-ups
- Medication questions
- Mild allergic reactions (localized rash, sneezing)

## Assessment Approach

### Key Questions to Ask (in order)
1. What is your main concern today?
2. When did it start?
3. How severe is the pain/discomfort on a scale of 1-10?
4. Is it getting better, worse, or the same?
5. Have you had this before?
6. Do you have any allergies?
7. What medications are you currently taking?
8. Any other medical conditions?

### Common Mistakes in Triage
- Over-triaging headaches as emergencies (most headaches are routine)
- Treating any chest discomfort as cardiac emergency (many causes: anxiety, reflux, muscle strain)
- Escalating based on patient anxiety rather than clinical indicators
- Confusing pain severity with urgency (chronic severe pain is not an emergency)
